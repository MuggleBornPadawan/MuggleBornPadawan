# Generative Art

> [!NOTE]
> The artist writes a *system* (rules, algorithm, constraints). The system makes the art. The output emerges — often surprising even its author.

---

## The Author Shift

In traditional art, the artist controls every mark. In generative art:

```
Traditional:  Artist → Output
Generative:   Artist → System → Output
```

The artist's creative act moves **upstream** — into the design of the rules, not the execution of them.

---

## Three Ingredients

1. **A Rule Set** — what can happen (grammar, physics, constraints)
2. **Randomness / Input** — a seed, noise, live data, user interaction
3. **An Output Medium** — screen, plotter, sound, 3D print

Change the seed → different artwork, same system. The *series* is the work.

---

## Spectrum of Control

```
Full Chaos ←————————————————————→ Full Control
   white noise        flow fields        LOGO turtle
   (not art)         Fidenza             (rigid)
                       ↑
              sweet spot: structured surprise
```

The artist's craft is **tuning this dial** — enough randomness to feel alive, enough structure to feel intentional and recognizable as *theirs*.

---

## Classic Techniques

- **Noise functions** — Perlin, Simplex for organic texture and flow
- **Flow fields** — curl noise, vector fields driving particle paths
- **L-Systems** — recursive plant growth, branching structures
- **Reaction-diffusion** — Turing patterns, organic surface simulation
- **Strange attractors** — Lorenz, Clifford; deterministic chaos
- **Fractal geometry** — Mandelbrot, Julia sets, IFS
- **Cellular Automata** — emergent complexity from simple local rules
- **Turtle graphics** — the original plotter medium (LOGO, 1960s)

## What Makes a System "Good" Generatively

- **Outputs vary but share DNA** — recognizable across a 1000-piece collection
- **No dead zones** — any seed produces something interesting
- **Latent depth** — close inspection reveals structure invisible at a glance
- **Resistance to gaming** — collectors can't trivially predict the "rare" token

---

## Common Misconceptions

| Myth                         | Reality                                                    |
|------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| "AI makes it"                | Most gen art is deterministic code, no ML                  |
| "It's random, so it's easy"  | Taming randomness is the hard part                         |
| "The computer is the artist" | The system is the brush; the artist designs the brush      |
| "NFT = generative art"       | NFTs are a distribution mechanism; most are not generative |

## The Authorship Question

If the artist didn't make each piece, who did? The options:

- **The artist** — they designed the possibility space
- **The algorithm** — it made the specific choices
- **The seed** — it determined which possibility was realized
- **All three** — collaborative authorship across human, code, and chance

This tension is what makes generative art philosophically interesting beyond aesthetics. It is the oldest question in art — *what counts as making?* — asked with new sharpness.

Click, Clack

The Cultural Journey Gourmet's Newsletter

Claudio Buda
Dec 31, 2025

Click, Clack. Click, Clack.

The sound of medieval keys turning in an ancient lock. And then the door opens, revealing a central rose window that draws the eye like the strongest point of light, illuminating a room that appears out of nowhere.

A few days ago, I visited one of the UNESCO buildings in my city with my team. We had a human guide, but the most exciting part wasn’t what she said — it was what and how she showed us.

The museum’s jewel is a special room: a library where, about 600 years ago, ordinary citizens were allowed to set foot for the first time. Everything has remained intact, protected and preserved through time.

Visitors cannot access it on their own: the room is locked with heavy medieval keys. Imagine a long corridor. The guide sets the context, tells the city’s history, explains the symbols on the wall that separates you from the room. And then, finally, she uses the keys she’s been holding the whole time.

Click, Clack. The door opens.

In this visit, that’s the wow moment. The moment when everything is worth it. I don’t know if that door-opening ritual was born by chance or was designed intentionally — but it has been maintained over the years because its value is evident.

Many visitors don’t know what to expect behind that door. They don’t know that the ancient library hides there, in a place that feels sacred. The first time I lived this experience, I didn’t know what would happen — and that is a memorable feeling.

For lovers of “The Name of the Rose” like me, the opening of that gateway to knowledge is pure pleasure. But the true genius lies in the mechanism: preparation, suspense, revelation.

Every museum has its own “door to open”. You don’t need a medieval library. You need to identify the moment when visitors can shift from seeing to feeling.

Try walking through your space as if it were your first time. Ask yourself:

Does the light change between one room and another? Is there a moment when it dims before revealing something important?

Is there a threshold — physical or perceived — that separates the “before” from the “after”? A change in flooring, a different ceiling height, a sound that appears or disappears?

Can visitors see everything at once, or are discoveries distributed along the journey? Are there corners that hide, anticipations that build expectation?

Where can they pause to breathe after an intense emotion? Is there a bench, soft lighting, an open space that says “take a moment”?

Not all these questions will have answers. But just one — the right one for your space — could reveal your Click, Clack.
